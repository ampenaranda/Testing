# The Andragogy Detective: Mystery of Adult Learning

An interactive detective game board designed to help faculty understand Malcolm Knowles' principles of Andragogy (adult learning theory).

## Overview

This educational game presents six "cases" that faculty members must solve by applying the correct adult learning principles. Each case represents one of Knowles' six core principles of Andragogy and challenges players to think critically about how adults learn differently from children.

## The Six Principles Covered

1. **Need to Know** - Adults need to understand why they need to learn something before undertaking to learn it
2. **Self-Concept** - Adults have a self-concept of being responsible for their own decisions and lives
3. **Experience** - Adults come with a greater volume and quality of experience that serves as a resource for learning
4. **Readiness to Learn** - Adults are ready to learn things that help them cope with real-life situations
5. **Orientation to Learning** - Adults are problem-centered rather than subject-centered in their learning
6. **Motivation** - Adults are more responsive to internal motivators than external ones

## How to Play

1. **Open the Game**: Simply open `andragogy-detective-game.html` in any modern web browser
2. **Read Each Case**: Each case presents a realistic scenario involving faculty development or adult learning
3. **Make Your Choice**: Select the approach that best applies the Andragogy principle for that case
4. **Learn from Feedback**:
   - Correct answers provide reinforcement and explanation
   - Incorrect answers offer learning opportunities with explanations of why the choice doesn't align with adult learning theory
5. **Track Progress**: Watch your progress bar fill as you solve cases
6. **Complete the Investigation**: Solve all six cases to master the principles of Andragogy!

## Educational Benefits

This game helps faculty:
- Understand the fundamental differences between pedagogy (child learning) and andragogy (adult learning)
- Apply adult learning principles to their own teaching practices
- Design more effective professional development programs
- Create better learning experiences for adult students
- Recognize why certain teaching approaches work better with adult learners

## Tips for Facilitators

- Allow participants to play individually first, then discuss cases as a group
- Use incorrect answers as discussion prompts about why they don't work
- Connect each principle to participants' real teaching experiences
- Follow up with action planning: how will they apply these principles?

## About Andragogy

Andragogy, developed by Malcolm Knowles in the 1970s, revolutionized our understanding of how adults learn. Unlike children, adults:
- Are self-directed and autonomous
- Bring valuable life experience to learning
- Need to see practical relevance
- Learn best when addressing real problems
- Are motivated by internal goals

Understanding these principles is essential for effective teaching in higher education, professional development, and workplace training.
